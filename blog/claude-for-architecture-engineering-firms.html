How Can Architecture and Engineering Firms Use Claude?

Most hours in an architecture or engineering office go to documents: specifications, codes, RFIs, submittals, meeting notes, reports. That is where Claude helps most. Here is what works, how to set it up, and where it must stop.

November 17, 2026 8 min read Guide
Quick answer

Architecture and engineering firms get the most from Claude on document work: comparing specifications against the brief, summarizing code sections you provide, drafting RFIs, submittal reviews, meeting minutes and reports, checking schedules and quantity spreadsheets, and reading drawings exported to PDF or images. Put each job's documents in a Claude project so every chat uses them, ask for quotes with sheet or section references, and turn on file creation to get Word, Excel, PowerPoint or PDF output. Claude does not replace a licensed professional: it holds no license, does not stamp drawings, and its calculations and code interpretations need review by a qualified person.

Can Claude read drawings?

It can read drawings you export to PDF or image files, with limits. The upload article lists the formats: PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML, ODT, RTF, EPUB, JSON and XLSX, plus JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP images. Native CAD and BIM model files are not on that list, so export sheets to PDF or images first.

  • Keep drawing sets short. Claude analyzes the visual content of PDFs of 100 pages or fewer. From 101 to 1,000 pages it reads the text only, which misses linework.
  • Send the sheets that matter. A focused set of plans, sections and details gets a better read than a full set.
  • Images are capped at 8000 by 8000 pixels. Crop to the area in question for small text and dimensions.
  • Verify every dimension it reports. Reading a scanned sheet is not measuring a model.

Text in Word files is extracted without embedded images. If the figures in a report matter, upload it as PDF.

How should a firm set this up?

  1. One project per job. A project holds the brief, specifications, standards you are licensed to use, meeting notes and correspondence, and its instructions can set your house style. Project files are limited to 30MB each. When a project grows past the context window, paid plans switch it to retrieval automatically (how that works).
  2. Write project instructions. For example: "Cite spec section and sheet number for every statement. If the documents do not answer the question, say so."
  3. Turn on file creation. Under Settings, Capabilities, enable code execution and file creation so Claude can produce Excel, Word, PowerPoint and PDF files. On Team and Enterprise, an Owner controls this for the organization.
  4. Connect where documents live. Connectors for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace let Claude search files you already keep there.
  5. Agree on review rules. Decide which outputs may go out after a quick check and which always need a senior review.

Where must Claude stop?

  • Licensed work stays licensed. Claude does not hold a professional license, cannot seal drawings and does not carry professional liability. Structural, fire, life-safety and code compliance decisions need a qualified person. Check your local rules on what must be reviewed by whom.
  • Calculations need checking. Ask Claude to show every step and formula, then verify the result in your own tools.
  • Codes change and vary by place. Claude's built-in knowledge stops at a cutoff date and may not know local amendments. Give it the exact edition you are designing to.
  • Confidentiality. Client documents often carry confidentiality terms. Anthropic says it does not train on inputs or outputs from commercial products such as Team and Enterprise by default, but your contracts may still limit what can be uploaded.
  • Files from outside can carry instructions. The file creation article warns that hidden instructions in external files or websites can try to manipulate Claude. Review output before it goes to a client.

FAQ

Can Claude open DWG or Revit files?

Those formats are not on the supported upload list. Export sheets to PDF or images, and schedules to Excel or CSV.

Can Claude check a design against the building code?

It can compare documents against code text you provide and flag likely issues. A qualified professional must make the compliance determination, using the edition and local amendments that apply.

Is a Team plan enough for a small practice?

Team adds shared projects and admin controls over capabilities and data settings. Whether you need Enterprise depends on your security, retention and sign-on requirements.

Will Claude keep our project details confidential?

By default, Anthropic does not train models on inputs or outputs from commercial plans. Retention and connector settings are for your administrators to configure, and client contracts still govern what you upload.
